**Accomplishments in discovery**

Dr. Davis's research begins from observed failures in real engineering
practice, studies those failures systematically, and translates the
resulting insights into practical interventions for developers,
infrastructure maintainers, and software-producing organizations. His
work is organized around three major discovery areas: regular
expression engineering, software infrastructure security, and reuse of
pre-trained deep neural network models.

Across these discovery areas, Davis has built a broad and productive
research record. His work spans software engineering, cybersecurity,
and systems, with publications at venues including ICSE, FSE, ASE,
ISSTA, EMSE, USENIX Security, IEEE S&P, CCS, EuroSys, USENIX ATC,
VLDB, and WWW. His record includes #NUM_PEER_REVIEWED_WORKS

*Regular expressions:* The work began in regular expression
engineering, Davis has helped establish Regular Denial of Service
(ReDoS) as a broad software engineering and security problem. His
work characterized how real regexes and regex engines depart from
textbook assumptions (@davis2019aren), showed that ReDoS is
widespread across mainstream engines and reusable software projects
(@davis2018impact), and developed practical defenses at both the
engine and application levels (@davis2021using, @hassan2023improving).
He contributed first characterization of ReDoS-inducing behavior due
to backreferences (@liu2026regular). This research area has produced
nine Tier-1 papers, including two distinguished paper awards
(@davis2018impact, @michael2019regexes), and has been supported by an
NSF SaTC Small award on which Davis served as PI
(@nsf-satc-regex-2022).

*Engineering with pre-trained models:* Davis was among the first
scholars to frame pre-trained model reuse as a software engineering
problem. His group created and analyzed early datasets of model
reuse, studied how engineers exchange and adapt pre-trained models,
characterized failures in model re-engineering and pre-trained model
reuse, and developed automated techniques for detecting metadata and
naming inconsistencies. This work treats models as reusable software
components that must be selected, adapted, exchanged, tested, and
deployed systematically. The research has produced six Tier-1 papers
and one provisional patent, and has helped define an emerging software
engineering research area around model reuse and AI supply chains.
See Section [X].

*Software infrastructure security:* Davis has advanced work on memory
safety in embedded software and software provenance through signing.
On memory safety, his group developed a systematic validation
approach for embedded network stacks and formalized "unit proofing"
as a pragmatic approach to bounded model checking for memory safety.
This work exposed defects in major open-source real-time operating
systems and produced practical guidance for formal verification
workflows. On software signing, Davis's group measured signing
adoption and quality, then studied the organizational, usability, and
deployment factors that shape adoption of signing technologies. This
work has produced six Tier-1 papers and has been supported by NSF,
Google, Rolls-Royce, Cisco, and OpenAI funding. See Section [X].
